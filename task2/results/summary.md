# Experiment results

Generated by `python experiments.py`.

- A *ply* is one move of one player. One game cycle = 2 plies (MAX moves, then MIN moves).
- **Nodes** = game-tree nodes visited (MAX nodes + MIN nodes, root included). This is the measure of time complexity; seconds are given next to it.
- **b\*** = effective branching factor: the b for which a uniform tree of the same depth has the same number of nodes, `nodes = 1 + b* + b*^2 + ... + b*^m`.
- **Max ply** = deepest level reached (m).
- **Peak memory** = most nodes held at once: the nodes on the current path plus the untried successors of each of them. Depth-first search keeps O(b·m) nodes, so this grows with m, not with the number of nodes visited. **TT** = transposition-table entries (stored states), the extra memory paid for the table.
- Per-search time budget: 30.0s.

## F. Map scaling: Heuristic Alpha-Beta from the opening position of generated maps

Point-symmetric maps built by `scaled_map(n)`. *Lane distance* = moves the tank needs to reach a cell with a clear firing line on the enemy base (the BFS distance used by EVAL). *BFS field* = time to compute one distance field over the whole grid, O(H·W), cached per wall layout.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7x7 | 49 | 6 | 7 | 0.052 | 577 | 1420 | 2.31 | 46 | 21.9 |
| 13x13 | 169 | 6 | 11 | 0.175 | 621 | 1280 | 2.28 | 49 | 25.6 |
| 25x25 | 625 | 6 | 19 | 0.631 | 621 | 1281 | 2.28 | 49 | 27.7 |
| 49x49 | 2401 | 6 | 35 | 2.446 | 621 | 1281 | 2.28 | 49 | 39.0 |

The number of nodes at a fixed cutoff depth and the peak memory do not grow with the map: they depend on b (at most 6 actions) and on the depth only. The distance to the targets grows with the map and soon exceeds the cutoff depth, so the search alone cannot see them; the BFS distance fields in EVAL (linear in the number of cells) guide the tank there.
